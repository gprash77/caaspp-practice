## ELA PT — “Should Our School Create a Student News Program?” — 3 items / 13 points

The original 1,100–1,350 word source package is balanced and fictional:

- Source 1 explains potential communication, speaking, writing, and teamwork benefits.
- Source 2 gives nonnegotiable privacy/permission rules plus schedule, equipment, captioning, supervision, and access constraints.
- Source 3 reports one pilot school's mixed first-year data: participation and audience growth alongside editing time, missed deadlines, and survey concerns.

The prompt permits yes, no, or a conditional position. Students never need personal media access, family technology, or prior broadcasting knowledge. Required accessibility and privacy safeguards are obligations rather than optional advantages.

## Test 2-informed anti-duplication and QA contracts

- No Test 1 Art Day, animal-surroundings, telephone, balloon, or official item content is reused.
- No Test 2 relay, mail, paper recycling, Moon observation, walking-route, outdoor-learning, canal-lock, bottle, or traffic item is adapted by substitution.
- No Grade 3 passage, listening, PT, or prompt shell is reused.
- Generic prompts such as “Answer both parts” and “What is the main idea of the presentation?” must include item-specific context so exact-prompt collision tests remain zero.
- All embedded data tables declare a semantic row header and render at their source location.
- Every listening item passes audio-only review with transcript and optional visual hidden.
- Every manual item has a registered versioned rubric whose maximum matches the item points.
- Objective keys, partial-credit tiers, unordered sets, ordered pairs, numeric equivalence, and declarative constraints receive positive, negative, malformed, and boundary tests.
- Test 3 remains unavailable until the independent fixture/eval, Test 1 and Test 2 regressions, full build, full browser suite, desktop visual inspection, and narrow-viewport checks pass.
